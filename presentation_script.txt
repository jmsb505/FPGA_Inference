[SLIDES 6-9] What is Volumetric Image Registration?
Title: Volumetric Image Registration Workflow
Time: 0:40 - 1:20 (40 seconds)

SPOKEN SCRIPT:
"In medical imaging, patient scans are taken using different modalities—such 
as MRI for soft tissue detail and CT for dense bone structures. Volumetric 
registration is the process of spatially aligning these two 3D scans so a surgeon 
or algorithm can view them in a single unified coordinate space.

Instead of relying on slow traditional optimization algorithms that take 
minutes per volume, we use a deep neural network. The network takes the 
moving MRI and fixed CT volumes, predicts a dense 3D deformation flow field, 
and warps the moving image into alignment in a single forward pass."

[SLIDE 12] Target Hardware Platform (Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC)
Title: Target Platform: Why an FPGA-based SoC?
Time: 2:05 - 2:35 (30 seconds)

SPOKEN SCRIPT:
"To deploy this in point-of-care medical environments under strict thermal 
power limits (under 15 Watts), we chose the AMD Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC.

This system combines a quad-core ARM CPU—which handles data management and 
pre/post-processing—with FPGA programmable logic hosting a specialized Deep 
Learning Processing Unit (DPU) core for hardware-accelerated tensor matrix operations."

[SLIDE 36] Vitis AI Deployment Flow
Title: Vitis AI Deployment Flow
Time: 2:50 - 3:25 (35 seconds)

SPOKEN SCRIPT:
"Before executing on the board, our trained 2.5D VoxelMorph model passes through 
the Vitis AI Deployment Flow, which consists of three key steps:

First, INT8 Quantization using vai_q_pytorch. We pass 100 representative 
calibration slice stacks through the model to convert 32-bit floating-point 
weights into 8-bit integers without requiring any retraining.

Second, Vitis AI Compilation using vai_c_pytorch. The compiler parses the 
quantized graph targeting our specific DPU architecture—the DPUCZDX8G B4096 core. 
It automatically maps all supported U-Net convolution layers onto the hardware DPU 
and generates the compiled binary .xmodel file.

Finally, Board Runtime Deployment. On the FPGA board, the Vitis AI Runtime (VART) 
loads the .xmodel file, allowing Python and PYNQ to feed slice stacks directly 
into the hardware DPU buffers.

Now, let’s look at how this compiled model actually executes in real time 
across the three orthogonal views..."


[SLIDES 37-43] DPU 2.5D Multi-View Fusion Execution
Title: DPU Execution: Three 2D Flows -> 3D Fusion
Time: 3:00 - 4:00 (60 seconds)

SPOKEN SCRIPT:
"Welcome back. Now let’s look at how our deployed model actually executes on the board.

Because the DPU hardware array is optimized for 2D convolutions, we reformulated 
3D registration into a 2.5D multi-view approach.

Instead of feeding a heavy 3D volume all at once, the ARM host extracts 2.5D slice 
stacks across three orthogonal planes: Axial, Coronal, and Sagittal.

The FPGA DPU hardware engine rapidly computes the 2D displacement field for 
each slice stack. The host CPU then projects these 2D vectors back into 3D 
space and computes a 3-view mean fusion flow field. This fusion recovers full 
3D spatial depth while keeping operational memory under 110 megabytes."


[SLIDE 44] Experimental Setup & Telemetry KPIs
Title: Experimental Setup
Time: 4:00 - 4:40 (40 seconds)

[SLIDES 46-50] Registration Quality & Parity Results
Title: Registration Quality After Deployment
Time: 5:05 - 6:20 (75 seconds)

SPOKEN SCRIPT:
"Here are the final registration quality results:

Starting from an unregistered baseline Dice score of 0.571, our 2.5D mean-fused 
INT8 model deployed on the FPGA DPU reaches a Dice score of 0.7647 and reduces 
Target Registration Error down to 2.02 millimeters.

Crucially, comparing our INT8 FPGA model against the 32-bit floating-point 
software reference running on CPU (0.7638 Dice), we observe zero loss in 
clinical registration accuracy. Furthermore, our 2.5D multi-view FPGA model 
actually matches full 3D desktop GPU models (0.7609 Dice) while consuming 
over 15 times less peak memory.

In summary, Vitis AI 8-bit quantization preserves clinical alignment quality 
perfectly on edge FPGA hardware."
